Gateway BP then LR: attB-GFP into pDEST-demo

Recombine the 751 bp between attB-GFP's attB sites into pDONR-demo, giving the entry clone pDONR-demo-attB-GFP; grow that up and recombine it into pDEST-demo. Two staged reactions, each with its own incubation, stop, transformation and plate.

GFP carries no att site, so it is amplified onto attB ends first: each primer is a whole tail -- 4 G residues, the 25 bp att site and the frame bases this fusion needs, 29 bases forward and 29 reverse -- over an annealing region read off the insert's own end. The G residues leave with the BP by-product, so the entry clone is the same record as one made from an insert that arrived attB-flanked.

BP runs first: the 751 bp between the attB sites move into pDONR-demo, making pDONR-demo-attB-GFP, which is picked, grown up and purified before LR takes it.

LR then moves the 751 bp between the entry clone's att sites into the 164 bp of destination vector outside its own, and the ccdB cassette leaves with the by-product.

The junction is not scarless: the clone gains a whole att site at each end of the insert, attB1 spelling ACAAGTTTGTACAAAAAAGCAGGCT and attB2 spelling ACCACTTTGTACAAGAAAGCTGGGT. A fusion reads through both.

GFP reads on the same strand as T7 promoter, 8 bp downstream of it.

There is a ribosome binding site annotated ahead of GFP, so the clone may make its protein.

Troubleshooting

No band
Drop the annealing temperature by 3 °C and check the GFP template is there.
Several bands
Raise the annealing temperature, or gel-purify the band of the right size.

Notes

  • Each primer carries a whole attB tail: 4 G residues, the 25 bp att site and the frame bases the fusion needs, 29 bases forward and 29 reverse. The manual's own cause of few or no colonies is a tail short of that.
  • The annealing temperature above is read from the annealing regions alone; a tail pairs with nothing on the template in the first cycles.
  • Over 70 bp of primer the manual switches to a two-step adapter PCR, which installs the same whole tail in two rounds and is not planned here.

Expected result

  • Clean DNA, free of polymerase, primers and dNTPs.

Troubleshooting

Low recovery
Elute twice through the same column, or pool two reactions before purifying.

Notes

  • The BP reaction takes purified attB DNA: gel-purifying the product is the manual's fix for few or no colonies, and it takes the attB primers and their dimers away.
  • An entry clone running as a 2.2 kb supercoiled plasmid is a BP reaction that cloned attB primer-dimers instead.

Expected result

  • A 10 µL reaction holding both DNAs.

The DNA does not fit the reaction volume
Concentrate either DNA, or scale the whole reaction up keeping the enzyme mix at its stated fraction of the volume.

Notes

  • The volumes above take each DNA at the concentration the manual's own table assumes; pipette what your prep needs for the picomoles and make the difference up with TE buffer, pH 8.0.
  • The manual fixes the picomoles and not the weight: 50 fmol of each, the attB DNA down to 20 fmol, weighed here from each record's own length.
  • Do not go over 250 ng of donor vector or 500 ng of DNA altogether: excess DNA inhibits the reaction.
  • A linear attB product and a supercoiled donor vector are the substrates the manual calls most efficient for BP.

Notes

  • Colour does not report this insertion; screen every colony by PCR.
  • Unreacted destination vector and the by-product both keep the ccdB gene, which kills TOP10 chemically competent E. coli, so they do not grow. A strain carrying F' would supply ccdA and cancel that.
  • The expression clone lost the chloramphenicol cassette with the by-product, so restreaking a colony on 30 µg/mL chloramphenicol confirms it: a true expression clone does not grow there, and one carrying a mutated ccdB gene does.
  • Small colonies beside large ones are usually unreacted pDONR-demo-attB-GFP co-transforming; restreak them on the entry clone's own antibiotic to tell.

Expected result

  • Each of the 2 junctions is read: the flanking pair crosses them all.
  • Correct clone: 871 bp.
  • Empty vector: 429 bp.

Troubleshooting

No band in any lane
The colony was too much material: touch a smaller one, or dilute it.
The plate carries large colonies and small ones
The small ones are usually unreacted pDONR-demo-attB-GFP co-transforming. It carries neither of these primers, so it adds no band here; restreak on the entry clone's own antibiotic to tell.

Notes

  • The long first step at 94 °C lyses the cells; there is no purified template.
  • Both primers sit in the destination vector's backbone, so one product crosses both attB junctions and an unrecombined vector gives a band of its own.
  • An unrecombined destination vector still carries ccdB, which kills TOP10 chemically competent E. coli, so its lane is what a strain supplying ccdA or a damaged ccdB gene would put on the plate.
  • The manual asks for a restriction digest alongside the first time this is run: mispriming and contaminating template both give artefacts.

Expected result

  • Sequencing forward anneals 103 bp from its own junction and has to read 854 bp to cover the far one.
  • Sequencing reverse anneals 117 bp from its own junction and has to read 868 bp to cover the far one.
  • The junctions read as ACAAGTTTGTACAAAAAAGCAGGCT and ACCACTTTGTACAAGAAAGCTGGGT, and the parts match pDONR-demo-attB-GFP.

Troubleshooting

The read starts too close to the junction
Move the primer further out; the first bases after a primer are unreadable.

Notes

  • Only a read across the junctions confirms the assembly, whatever the screen said.
  • A provider whose read is shorter than the lengths above needs a further primer inside the inserts.
  • No vendor primer reads these junctions: GW1 and GW2 are suitable for pCR8/GW/TOPO alone, and an M13 primer crosses at least 130 bp of vector first. Both primers above are designed against this record.
  • Send at least 500 ng of plasmid with 5-50 pmol of each primer.
