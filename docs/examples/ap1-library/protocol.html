Library assembly: 3 part lists into DMX-demo

Join 72 synthesised parts into 13824 distinct constructs in 3 rounds. Each round opens the library with BbsI, releases one part list with BsaI, ligates the two, and transforms, grows and preps the result for the round after it.

One round appends one part list to every member of the library at once, so 3 rounds make 13824 constructs out of 72 synthesised parts.

The product keeps BbsI's sites, which is what lets the next round open it, and loses BsaI's with the external stuffers.

Your vector already carried an internal stuffer, so every part enters position N on AGGA, which is the overhang that stuffer spells: DNA that exists cannot be re-chosen.

The overhang standard changes 27 amino acid(s) across 26 part end(s); changes.tsv has wild type beside synthesised, and that is what you are about to pay for.

The last round needs 4,147,200 colonies for 300x coverage of its 13,824 products. A round short of that loses members no later round can put back.

The finished barcode block is 41 bp and reads in the reverse of the order the rounds ran; barcodes.tsv says which barcode names which part.

Troubleshooting

No band
Drop the annealing temperature by 3 °C and check the pool went in.
A hump after the peak on a Bioanalyzer or TapeStation trace
Heteroduplexes, which is what over-amplification leaves. Run it again with fewer cycles. What too many cycles cost is dropout, chimeras and polymerase error; pooling the blocks equimolar later restores the evenness but none of those.

Notes

  • Every pair anneals at 65.1 °C, so one block of tubes takes them all.
  • Twist's band for a 350 nt pool is 12 to 14 cycles; Twist Oligo Pools Amplification Protocol DOC-4060 REV 1.0 gives the same three bands. Its FAQ answers that more cycles give worse uniformity, so 12 is what prints.
  • The outer primer is what makes a batch a batch: it is dropped at PCR2, so a block cannot be pulled out of a batch it does not sit in.
